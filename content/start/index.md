# Start with Orlo

Choose the path that matches the work you want to do. You can add the other paths later. [How Orlo works](/start/how-orlo-works) shows where task requests, agent permissions, and your model provider fit together.

:::cards
- [Test an AI task](/start/first-task) Compare models on a built-in example and send a checked request. Needs an admin and provider credentials.
- [Connect an agent](/start/first-agent) Check that a tool is blocked, then allowed after a permission change. No model call is needed.
- [Use shared skills](/start/team-skills) Load published instructions in your AI app. Needs access to your organization’s skills.
:::

Invited to review work? [Open and review your assignment](/start/review-work).

## Open your workspace

[Sign up or accept an invitation](/start/sign-up-and-keys). In the console, the welcome card helps you choose a path and return to saved work. To reopen it, open your profile menu and choose **Getting started**. It shows the paths your access permits.

You do not need a runtime key to browse the console or connect your own AI app. Applications and agent SDKs use runtime keys; an admin supplies them with the required scopes.

## What Orlo connects

A task defines a job, such as routing support tickets or summarizing a policy. Comparisons test models on examples of that job. A release pins the task instructions and rules used for requests. Agent permissions decide submitted tool calls. Shared skills distribute versioned instructions to tasks, agents, and employees’ AI apps.

Production answers, permission decisions, approvals, and retrievals become records your team can inspect. Reviewed corrections and saved steps can become examples for the next comparison. [Follow that loop](/console/tasks/improve-from-production).

[Concepts](/start/concepts) explains the terms. [Who does what](/start/who-does-what) explains access. If a step does not work, see [Troubleshooting](/start/troubleshooting).
